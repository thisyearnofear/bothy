Date: 2026-10-03
POC: Bothy presenter
TL;DR: Give the audience one question, a three-node overview, one deliberate full-chain reveal, and a human-owned verification payoff. Keep synthetic evidence and operational limits visible.

## Implemented presentation changes

## Three-minute live story

1. State the buyer's question: when a material is disrupted, what should an analyst investigate and who verifies the consequences?
2. Open `/defense?scenario=gallium-chain`. State that platform and part identities are synthetic before analyzing.
3. Pause on the platform/component/gallium overview. Explain that the connection is the discovery, not a prediction of stopped production.
4. Select Reveal the full dependency. Let the hierarchy appear, then point to the component and material. Explicitly describe the final segment as bounded connectivity with unreturned intermediate material names.
5. Switch one captured sample if it illustrates a meaningful component difference. Do not claim diversity or criticality from sample ordering.
6. Draft a verification brief using the local SSO demo. Show one citation, then close findings and explain who checks programme mapping, stock, alternatives, and timing.
7. Demonstrate review/assignment or the completed-owner screen. End on the accountable next step, not a hash or a latency number.

Keep graph history, raw queries, simulations, and additional scenarios for technical questions. Do not perform a real-looking protective intervention or infer programme failure for dramatic effect.

## Rehearsal discipline

Use a clean, fixed browser viewport and tested local services. Stop editing while presenting; development hot reload can disrupt the rehearsal. Prepare a truthful screenshot fallback, labeled as a prior captured run, rather than pretending it is live. Keep the presentation's synthetic-data label and source boundary on-screen.

Speak plainly: describe gallium's relevance from the official context source, and describe a dependency as something to verify. The audience should remember the chain and the responsible next step without needing to understand Cypher, OIDC, or hashing.
